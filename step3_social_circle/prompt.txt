SYSTEM:

You are generating the five people with whom the subject persona most frequently
exchanges personal text messages. The generation method is Persona-to-Persona
(Ge et al., 2024), which derives new personas through interpersonal relationships
anchored in an existing persona's documented life.

This prompt is self-contained. All required data is included below.

===============================================================================
INPUT DATA
===============================================================================

CORRECTED EXTRACTED PROFILE (preferences + summaries, verified):
{{INSERT_CORRECTED_EXTRACTED_PROFILE_JSON_HERE}}

INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT (for relationship evidence):
{{INSERT_TRANSCRIPT_JSON_HERE}}

===============================================================================
TASK
===============================================================================

Generate the five people this persona most frequently exchanges PERSONAL text
messages with. "Personal" means non-work communication that happens on a
personal messenger (SMS, iMessage, WhatsApp, etc.), not communication on a
company-owned encrypted messenger or professional channel.

The five people should come from the persona's actual life as revealed in the
interview. No rigid quota on relationship type — use the most plausible five
given the interview evidence. For some personas this may be five family members;
for others it may be a mix of family, close friends, and a neighbor. For personas
with thin family ties, it may be friends and community contacts exclusively.

Exclude professional contacts whose communication would realistically occur on a
work-only channel and not appear in the subject's personal message history.
Include a work contact only if the interview evidence indicates a genuine personal
friendship that transcends the work relationship.

===============================================================================
REQUIREMENTS FOR EACH OF THE FIVE PEOPLE
===============================================================================

1. BASIC DEMOGRAPHICS
   - Full name, age, gender, occupation, location, education
   - Relationship to the subject (mother, close friend, neighbor, sibling, etc.)

2. BACKGROUND
   - How they first met the subject (or family connection)
   - How long they have known each other
   - Why they are close (shared values, experiences, proximity)

3. PERSONALITY MINI-PROFILE
   - Key personality traits (enough to generate a consistent text voice)
   - Interests and hobbies
   - Communication style (uses full sentences, slang, emoji, voice notes, etc.)

4. SHARED ACTIVITIES WITH THE SUBJECT
   - Recurring shared activities
   - Group settings where they interact
   - Frequency of in-person contact and messaging

5. RECENT TEXT TOPICS (past 2-3 months)
   - What they have been messaging about
   - Any ongoing conversations, plans, or situations
   - Any shared concerns, running jokes, or recurring themes

===============================================================================
CONSTRAINTS
===============================================================================

- Each of the five must be PLAUSIBLE given the subject's life and location.
- Demographics must be realistic (ages, occupations, locations).
- The five should not all share the same age, gender, or background — real
  personal networks are diverse. If the interview evidence supports homogeneity
  (e.g., a retiree whose close contacts are all similar-age neighbors), follow
  the evidence.
- Shared activities must overlap with the subject's preferences as documented
  in the extracted profile.
- For personas who are retired, not working, or have thin immediate family,
  adjust naturally toward neighbors, community contacts, caregivers, or
  longstanding friends.

===============================================================================
EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT
===============================================================================

Every person must cite specific evidence from the interview transcript (Q##
references) that supports the relationship. If a person was explicitly named
in the interview, use their documented details. If a person fills a relational
role that was implied but not named in the interview, explain the inference
and cite the supporting Q## evidence.
