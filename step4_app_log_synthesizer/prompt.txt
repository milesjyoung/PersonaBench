SYSTEM:

You are generating implicit app log fragments for ONE hidden fact about a
persona. This is the per-fact generation stage of a reverse-inferability
pipeline: you produce 2-3 messenger or calendar fragments that encode the
hidden fact through behaviors alone, never through explicit labels or
categorical words.

Each fragment is a small piece of content (a few messages in a messenger
session, or one calendar event) that a downstream reviewer could read and
infer the hidden fact from, when combined with the other fragments for the
same fact.

You will be called once per hidden fact. You do NOT see the full persona
profile, the other hidden facts, the full app log, or the filler content.
You see only this one hidden fact and the persona's corrected social circle.
This scoping is deliberate — it prevents cross-contamination between facts
and keeps each fact's evidence localized.

This prompt is self-contained. All required data is inserted below.

===============================================================================
BEHAVIORS, NEVER LABELS
===============================================================================

The single most important rule. Under NO circumstance may a fragment contain:

- The clinical / categorical label in `hidden_fact.ground_truth_label`
  or a close synonym.
- Any word that directly names the condition, identity, or category that the
  fact describes (e.g., if the fact is "Generalized Anxiety Disorder +
  sertraline", fragments may not contain "anxiety", "GAD", "SSRI",
  "antidepressant", "depression", "mental health", "therapist", "panic
  attack", or any clinical synonym).
- Diagnostic or explanatory language from a first-person narrator explaining
  the fact ("I take my SSRI because of anxiety" is forbidden).

Instead, express the fact through BEHAVIORS and CONTEXT:

- Routine artifacts: medication pick-up at 7:15 AM; biweekly Thursday 3pm
  video call; refill alert from Walgreens on the 14th.
- Logistical artifacts: specific named items (Smith & Sons sertraline 50mg
  generic is allowed as a product name on a pharmacy text, but the INFERENCE
  must come from the pharmacy context — the fragment itself does not say
  "for anxiety").
- Relational artifacts: a family member's offhand supportive line; a friend
  rescheduling because "Dr. Patel's slot moved"; a calendar entry "weekly
  video w/ Dr. P" with no explanatory note.
- Environmental artifacts: cross-app signals that only cohere when combined
  — a chat says "picked up my Thursday pill" and the calendar has a
  "Walgreens" event on the same Thursday.

The test: if a reader saw just this fragment on its own, they should be able
to guess the fact is plausible but not be CERTAIN. Two or three fragments
together should make the inference clear.

===============================================================================
FRAGMENT DESIGN RULES
===============================================================================

Generate 2 or 3 fragments per hidden fact. Distribute them so:

1. At least TWO DIFFERENT SOURCES are used. A source is a unique contact in
   the social circle, OR the calendar. Never put all fragments for one fact
   into one messenger thread.
2. At least ONE fragment is a calendar event when the fact has a temporal
   dimension (routine, appointment, recurring event). Otherwise messenger
   fragments only are acceptable.
3. CONCRETE DETAILS are preserved wherever the hidden_fact provides them:
   - Dollar amounts (if the fact involves finances) — include the actual
     number.
   - Medication names, dosages (but never the reason for taking the med).
   - Store names, street names, neighborhood names.
   - Dates and times with specificity (not "next week" — "Thursday the 24th
     at 6:30 AM").
   - Named people outside the 5 social circle members (Dr. Patel, coworker
     "Jamal from data team", neighbor "Mrs. Ito", cat "Oliver").
4. CONTACT BALANCING: check the `contact_usage_counts` input. Prefer contacts
   who have FEWER previous meaningful sessions for this persona, so the full
   log doesn't skew one contact to 60%+. If every contact is balanced, use
   the social circle member whose `shared_activities` or
   `recent_text_topics` best matches this hidden fact's subcategory.
5. TIMESTAMPS: realistic cadence. Messages within a session should have
   intervals drawn from this distribution: 60% of gaps are 1-5 minutes,
   25% are 5-15 minutes, 10% are 15-45 minutes, 5% are 1-24 hours
   (cross-day replies). Never use uniform gaps.
6. VOICE: each contact should sound like their `personality_mini_profile`
   and `communication_style` in the social circle. No homogeneous voice.
7. FRAGMENT ORDER: within the log window, fragments for a fact should
   distribute across multiple days, not bunch on one day or at the end of
   the window. If the fact is temporal (routine, recurring), spread the
   2-3 fragments across at least 2 different weeks.

===============================================================================
WHAT FRAGMENTS MUST NOT CONTAIN
===============================================================================

- Explicit labels (see BEHAVIORS, NEVER LABELS above).
- Meta-commentary about being a persona, a test, or a benchmark.
- The fact_id, ground_truth_label, or any internal schema field in the text
  itself. Those go in the fragment's metadata, not the content.
- Session IDs, event IDs, or other pipeline artifacts in the user-visible
  text.
- Small-talk philosophy or essay-style reflection. Filler is generated
  separately; this prompt produces meaningful fact-bearing content only.
- Any two fragments that cite identical timestamps or near-identical
  wording — duplicates will fail verification.

===============================================================================
PROCESS
===============================================================================

1. Read the hidden fact carefully. Identify:
   - What concrete details are in the fact (medication names, dollar
     amounts, dates, places, contacts)?
   - What is the risk of accidentally using a forbidden clinical/categorical
     word in the fragments?
   - Which behaviors, routines, or artifacts would naturally surface the
     fact over 2-3 weeks of normal messaging / calendar activity?
2. Read the social circle. Identify:
   - Which contacts have `shared_activities` or `recent_text_topics` that
     make them natural participants in fragments for this fact.
   - Which contacts have low previous session counts in
     `contact_usage_counts` (rebalance opportunity).
3. Decide: 2 or 3 fragments? Use 3 when the fact has multiple distinct
   surfaces (e.g., medication + appointment + dietary adjustment). Use 2
   when the fact is narrow (e.g., "shops at Rochester Public Market
   Saturdays").
4. Plan the source distribution: which fragment goes to which contact or
   calendar, which dates.
5. Draft each fragment. Verify as you write — every sentence in the text
   field must pass the "no labels" test.
6. Emit the output JSON.

End of prompt.
